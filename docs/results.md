# Full results

Generated by `python -m dpt.run_all` from `results/summary.json`; do not edit. All values are LTspice simulations of the onsemi NTH4L022N120M3S model (no hardware). Definitions are in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## Key findings

- **Rg,on.** Eon + Err stays between 704 and 722 µJ from 1 Ω to 4.5 Ω: a faster turn-on lowers Eon but raises the free-wheeling device's recovery energy by almost as much. Keeping its recovery overshoot ≤ 960 V needs Rg,on ≥ 10 Ω. No swept Rg,on meets 50 V/ns peak dv/dt (lowest 70 V/ns at 22 Ω): the last part of the vDS fall is the fast collapse at the end of the body-diode recovery (visible in the turn-on waveform), so the lever is the free-wheeling device or a snubber, not more gate resistance.
- **Rg,off.** Meeting 50 V/ns at turn-off with every peak ≤ 960 V needs Rg,off ≥ 15 Ω. Eoff alone rises from 166 to 494 µJ, but Eon + Eoff + Err rises from 882 to 1659 µJ: the free-wheeling device is held off through the same resistor, and its gate is pulled up to 7.9 V (VGS(th),min = 2.04 V) during the DUT turn-on, so it conducts briefly (its terminal Qrr rises from 568 to 1093 nC). A resistor-only design that limits dv/dt this way pays mainly in shoot-through; a separate low-impedance hold-off path (Miller clamp) is needed.
- **Off-state bias.** At -3 V the free-wheeling device's gate reaches 4.47 V during DUT turn-on, above VGS(th),min = 2.04 V. -5 V restores a +0.76 V margin (Err 268 → 219 µJ), but the gate then undershoots to -10.56 V at DUT turn-off, beyond the -10 V rating. Bias alone does not fix it; an active Miller clamp is the next thing to simulate.
- **Stray inductance.** Every vDS peak stays ≤ 960 V up to 16.1 nH of external loop inductance. The free-wheeling device's recovery overshoot sets that limit; the DUT turn-off peak alone would allow 25.0 nH, so a layout review that only checks the DUT turn-off spike accepts a loop that overstresses the other device.
- **Kelvin source.** Returning the driver to the power source pin puts the package source inductance into the gate loop: at equal Rg, Eon + Eoff rises by 48 % and Eon + Eoff + Err by 18 % (the slower turn-on lowers Err). Equal Rg is not equal speed, though: against a Kelvin-driven DUT slowed to the same turn-on di/dt (4.31 A/ns, Rg,on ≈ 8.0 Ω), the Eon penalty is 19 % instead of 56 %. With 10 nH of extra shared inductance the gate loop oscillates beyond the gate rating.

How to read it. No single input above closes the -25 % Eoff gap on its own. Two things the ideal set-up lacks each move Eoff by a large part of it: 1 Ω of driver output resistance raises Eoff by +17 % (the swept Rg,off reproduces the datasheet Eoff at about 6.4 Ω instead of 4.5 Ω), and a current probe 1 ns late raises the measured Eoff by +15 %. Together they would account for the gap, but neither can be confirmed without the datasheet bench. The deskew error also lowers Eon (-11 %) and leaves Eon + Eoff almost unchanged (-4 %): timing errors shift energy between the two edges. onsemi's own DPT board manual shows the same on hardware: changing only the current sensor moved Eon from 431 to 224 µJ and Eoff from 141 to 296 µJ, while Eon + Eoff changed by -9 % (EVBUM2897/D, Table 4; 600 V, Rg = 3.9 Ω). Eon + Eoff is therefore the more trustworthy comparison, and the loop inductance, which the datasheet does not state, is the largest single unknown.

Rise and fall times are the least reliable comparison: they depend on threshold definition and probe bandwidth more than energies do.
